\chapter*{Before We Begin}

This book is about a machine that weighs a kilogram and a half, runs on twenty watts, like a dim light bulb, and still recognizes faces, learns languages, and catches a flying ball. Your brain. We will look at it the way an engineer looks at an unfamiliar circuit board: what the parts are, how they are connected, what signals run along the wires, and what the circuit computes.

You will need neither formulas nor any knowledge of biology. You will need curiosity and a willingness to picture a wire, a light bulb, a switch. We will pick up everything else along the way.

The book has a second, full version, with equations, models, exercises, and references to the experiments. The chapters of the two versions come in the same order and carry the same numbers. If a chapter hooks you, open its full version: there you will find where each claim comes from and how to check it yourself. On the book's website every chapter switches between ``Short'' and ``Full'' with a single button.

One warning. We do not know everything about the brain, far from it, and I will say honestly where the measurements end and the guesswork begins. This is not a flaw of the book but the most interesting thing in it: many chapters end with questions nobody can answer yet.
